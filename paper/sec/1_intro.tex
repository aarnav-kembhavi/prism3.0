Converting document images into faithful, machine-readable structure --- body text in reading order, formulas as \LaTeX{}, tables as HTML --- underpins retrieval-augmented generation, digital archiving, accessibility, and the construction of training corpora for foundation models. The current state of the art is held by specialized vision--language models (VLMs) of 0.9B--241B parameters~\cite{paddleocrvl,mineru25,glmocr}, and by GPU pipeline systems such as MinerU~\cite{mineru} and Marker~\cite{marker} that chain multi-hundred-megabyte detectors and recognizers, typically including a VLM component for OCR or formulas. Both families presuppose datacenter or at least discrete-GPU deployment.

A large class of real deployments cannot meet that presupposition: on-premise processing of confidential documents, batch digitization on commodity office hardware, edge and offline settings, and cost-sensitive high-volume ingestion. For these, the relevant question is not \emph{what is the best score achievable}, but \emph{how much of that score survives a hard budget} --- no GPU, a few gigabytes of RAM, seconds per page.

This paper answers that question with PRISM, a document parsing pipeline designed under three simultaneous constraints: (i)~CPU-only execution, (ii)~total model weights under 300\,MB, and (iii)~single-digit seconds per page. PRISM contains no generative model larger than 20M parameters. Its accuracy instead comes from an unusually careful orchestration layer around four small specialists --- a layout detector that also predicts reading order (RT-DETR, 124\,MB ONNX), a text OCR stack (31\,MB), a distilled formula recognizer (Texo, 20M parameters), and a table-structure model (SLANet-plus, 7.4\,MB) --- with deterministic geometric reasoning doing the work that end-to-end systems leave to attention: formula band-splitting by ink profiles, fraction-bar fusion, dense-grid ``answer key'' detection, spanning-cell resolution, verification-gated routing around recognizer blind spots, and recognition-verified normalization of camera-captured pages (\cref{sec:normalization}).

On OmniDocBench v1.6~\cite{omnidocbench} --- the de facto standard benchmark, 1651 pages across nine real-world document types in English and Chinese, scored by the official public harness --- PRISM reaches an overall score of \textbf{86.3} (median 5.9\,s/page, 2.7\,GB peak RAM): above the 7B-parameter GPU VLM class (olmOCR-7B 85.7, Mistral OCR 85.7), 7.9 points ahead of the widely deployed Marker pipeline (78.4), and within 0.12 of the MinerU GPU pipeline (86.5) --- with formula recognition 4.5 points ahead of it --- while every system above it uses at least an order of magnitude more compute. On the v1.5 cut PRISM scores \textbf{88.1}: the highest pipeline score in our comparison, 1.4 above PP-StructureV3 (86.7) and past Gemini-2.5 Pro (88.0), Qwen2.5-VL-72B (87.0) and Deepseek-OCR (87.0). In a controlled same-machine CPU comparison, PRISM dominates the sub-300M ``efficient VLM'' class (SmolDocling~\cite{smoldocling}, GraniteDocling) on every accuracy metric while running $8$--$14\times$ faster in $0.6\times$ the memory.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A complete, reproducible recipe} for CPU-only document parsing at 283\,MB reaching 7B-VLM-class accuracy, including all routing, thresholding, and post-processing logic, validated end-to-end on the official OmniDocBench harness.
  \item \textbf{Recognition-verified normalization}: camera-capture corrections (shadow flattening, glare inpainting, rectification, contrast) applied as \emph{proposals} gated by a 10\,MB text-detection probe. We show lighting defects and page design are statistically inseparable at the pixel level --- benchmark slide gradients score \emph{higher} on shadow statistics than real shadows --- so any open-loop policy either misses defects or damages clean pages; closing the loop resolves each case by whether the page actually reads better.
  \item \textbf{Deterministic structure recovery} that substitutes for model capacity: ink-mass formula grouping (display-formula CDM $56.9 \rightarrow 85.4$ with zero added parameters), detector-native reading order --- including the counterintuitive result that better detection makes geometric ordering \emph{worse} --- and spanning-cell table assembly.
  \item \textbf{A perturbation audit of the benchmark itself}: re-scoring semantically-null transformations of our predictions shows the official harness is robust to styling and faithful marginalia but moves by half a leaderboard step on segmentation granularity alone (\cref{sec:perturb}); together with the evaluation-interaction bugs we quantify and fix (matcher pairing, delimiter corruption, un-compilable macros), this argues scores at the $\leq$0.5-point scale reflect convention as much as capability. We report every negative result with numbers.
  \item \textbf{An efficiency frontier study} placing pipelines and small VLMs on the same axes --- accuracy, latency, peak RAM, model size --- on identical hardware.
\end{itemize}
