\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

PRISM's residual gap to GPU systems is concentrated where model capacity is genuinely required: Chinese-embedded formulas (CDM 71 vs.\ 90 English even after the OCR-hybrid escape hatch --- the 20M recognizer's CJK coverage), handwriting (notes text edit $\sim$0.10 but near-zero formula recognition), inline mathematics read as Unicode text, and cell-content fidelity in dense newspaper listings (TEDS 68 there vs.\ 80 overall). These would be addressed by a larger recognizer or higher-resolution detection, both of which trade directly against the budget that defines the system. Our evaluation is also benchmark-centric: OmniDocBench's matcher-based scoring shapes some design choices (marginalia suppression, plain-text emphasis) that a different downstream consumer might weigh differently, though we keep all such behaviors behind flags.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented PRISM, a CPU-only document parsing pipeline with 283\,MB of models that reaches an OmniDocBench v1.6 overall score of 85.8 at 5.6 seconds per page --- above 7B-parameter GPU vision--language models, 7 points beyond the Marker pipeline, the top-scoring pipeline on the v1.5 snapshot ahead of PP-StructureV3, and dominant over the sub-300M VLM class on accuracy and efficiency simultaneously. The central lesson is that the orchestration layer --- geometric reasoning over ink, class-aware detection post-processing, evaluation-aware emission --- recovers most of the accuracy commonly attributed to scale, and that rigorous per-failure measurement (including of the evaluation pipeline itself) is the highest-leverage tool in building such systems. We release code, configuration and the full experiment log, including all negative results.
