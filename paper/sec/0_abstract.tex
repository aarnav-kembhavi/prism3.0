\begin{abstract}
   Document parsing --- converting page images into structured Markdown, HTML and \LaTeX{} --- is dominated by vision--language models with billions of parameters and by GPU pipeline stacks measured in gigabytes. We present PRISM, a document parsing pipeline whose entire model inventory is 283\,MB and which runs exclusively on CPU at a median of 3.0 seconds per page in a 2.6\,GB working set. PRISM combines a single RT-DETR layout detector, lightweight OCR, a 20M-parameter distilled formula recognizer, and a 7\,MB table-structure model, orchestrated by deterministic geometric algorithms: ink-mass formula grouping and splitting, recursive XY-cut reading order, and spanning-cell table assembly. For camera-captured inputs we introduce \emph{recognition-verified normalization}: every photometric or geometric correction is treated as a proposal that is accepted only when a lightweight text-detection probe confirms the page reads better, which resolves the inherent ambiguity between lighting defects and page design that defeats open-loop heuristics. On the OmniDocBench v1.6 benchmark (1651 pages, official harness), PRISM attains an overall score of 83.6 --- at parity with 3B-parameter GPU vision--language models (POINTS-Reader 83.4, Nanonets-OCR-s 83.6) and 5.1 points beyond the Marker pipeline --- while using orders of magnitude less compute than every system above it. We provide component-level ablations of every design decision, an honest account of negative results, and a controlled same-machine CPU comparison against sub-300M VLMs (SmolDocling, GraniteDocling) and PP-StructureV3, in which PRISM is simultaneously the most accurate and the most efficient sub-4\,GB system. Our results demonstrate that careful orchestration of small specialist models recovers most of the accuracy commonly assumed to require large generative models.
\end{abstract}
