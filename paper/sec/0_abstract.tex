\begin{abstract}
   Document parsing --- converting page images into structured Markdown, HTML and \LaTeX{} --- is dominated by vision--language models with billions of parameters and by GPU pipeline stacks measured in gigabytes. We present PRISM, a document parsing pipeline whose entire model inventory is under 250\,MB and which runs exclusively on CPU, at a median of under five seconds per page and a peak working set of 2.2\,GB. PRISM combines a single RT-DETR layout detector, lightweight OCR, a 20M-parameter distilled formula recognizer, and a 7\,MB table-structure model, orchestrated by a set of deterministic geometric algorithms: ink-mass formula grouping and splitting, recursive XY-cut reading order, token-to-grid table assembly, and a defect-normalization front end for camera captures. On the OmniDocBench v1.6 benchmark (1651 pages, official harness), PRISM attains an overall score of 78.5\,\todo{v14}, exceeding the Marker pipeline and approaching GPU pipeline systems, while using two orders of magnitude less compute than the vision--language models above it. We provide a component-level ablation of every design decision, an honest account of negative results, and a controlled same-machine CPU comparison against sub-300M VLMs (SmolDocling, GraniteDocling) and PP-StructureV3, in which PRISM is simultaneously the most accurate and the most efficient sub-4\,GB system. Our results demonstrate that careful engineering of small specialist models recovers most of the accuracy that is commonly assumed to require large generative models, and establishes a strong efficiency frontier for document AI on commodity hardware.
\end{abstract}
