\begin{abstract}
   Document parsing --- converting page images into structured Markdown, HTML and \LaTeX{} --- is dominated by vision--language models with billions of parameters and by GPU pipeline stacks measured in gigabytes. We present PRISM, a document parsing pipeline whose entire model inventory is 283\,MB and which runs exclusively on CPU at a median of 5.6 seconds per page in a 2.6\,GB working set. PRISM combines a single RT-DETR layout detector that also predicts reading order, lightweight OCR, a 20M-parameter distilled formula recognizer, and a 7\,MB table-structure model, orchestrated by deterministic geometric algorithms: ink-mass formula grouping and splitting, spanning-cell table assembly, and verification-gated routing around each recognizer's blind spots. For camera-captured inputs we introduce \emph{recognition-verified normalization}: every photometric or geometric correction is treated as a proposal that is accepted only when a lightweight text-detection probe confirms the page reads better, which resolves the inherent ambiguity between lighting defects and page design that defeats open-loop heuristics. On the OmniDocBench v1.6 benchmark (1651 pages, official harness), PRISM attains an overall score of 85.8 --- above 7B-parameter GPU vision--language models (olmOCR-7B 85.7, Mistral OCR 85.7), 7.3 points beyond the Marker pipeline, and within 0.7 of the multi-gigabyte MinerU GPU pipeline --- while using orders of magnitude less compute than every system above it. On the v1.5 cut it scores 87.1, ahead of every pipeline we measure against, including PP-StructureV3 (86.7). We provide component-level ablations of every design decision, an honest account of negative results, and a controlled same-machine CPU comparison against sub-300M VLMs (SmolDocling, GraniteDocling) and PP-StructureV3, in which PRISM is simultaneously the most accurate and the most efficient sub-4\,GB system. Our results demonstrate that careful orchestration of small specialist models recovers most of the accuracy commonly assumed to require large generative models.
\end{abstract}
