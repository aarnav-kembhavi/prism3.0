\begin{abstract}
   Document parsing --- converting page images into structured Markdown, HTML and \LaTeX{} --- is dominated by vision--language models with billions of parameters and by GPU pipeline stacks measured in gigabytes. We present PRISM, a document parser whose entire model inventory is 283\,MB and which runs exclusively on CPU at a median of 5.9 seconds per page in a 2.7\,GB working set. PRISM rests on two ideas. The first is \emph{verification-gated orchestration}: four small specialists (a layout detector that also predicts reading order, lightweight OCR, a 20M-parameter formula recognizer, a 7\,MB table-structure model) are composed by deterministic geometric reasoning, and wherever a specialist has a known blind spot, its output is routed or replaced only when a cheap recognition probe confirms the alternative reads better. The second applies the same principle to input pixels: in \emph{recognition-verified normalization}, every photometric or geometric correction of a camera capture is a proposal accepted only if a 10\,MB text-detection probe scores the corrected page higher. This closed loop resolves an ambiguity that defeats open-loop enhancement --- benchmark slide gradients score \emph{higher} on shadow statistics than real shadows --- and surfaces a finding we believe is of independent interest: modern mobile OCR is nearly invariant to photometric defects, so unconditional correction taxes every clean page while verified correction gets the rare genuine rescues for free. On OmniDocBench v1.6 (1651 pages, official harness), PRISM reaches an overall score of 86.3: above 7B-parameter GPU vision--language models, within 0.12 of the multi-gigabyte MinerU GPU pipeline while beating its formula recognition by 4.5 points, and ahead of every system its size by double digits. On the v1.5 cut it is the highest-scoring pipeline at 88.1, above PP-StructureV3 and Gemini-2.5 Pro. We report component-level ablations for every design decision, all negative results, a perturbation audit of the benchmark itself, and a controlled same-machine CPU comparison in which PRISM is simultaneously the most accurate and the most efficient sub-4\,GB system. Our results argue that verified composition of small specialists recovers most of the capability commonly attributed to scale.
\end{abstract}
